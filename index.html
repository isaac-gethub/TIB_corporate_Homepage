THE NEXT FIVE YEARS

Neither career is being automated away

Both are being reshaped around a narrower, harder version of the same job: judging what an automated system got wrong.

SOX/ITGC + AI

Continuous controls monitoring and AI-assisted testing will keep taking over the mechanical parts of the job — pulling populations, running samples, matching evidence to a control description. What AI won't do on its own is notice that a "clean" result is clean because the test is pointed at the wrong population, or that a control which has passed every quarter for two years has quietly stopped testing anything real. The controls professional's value shifts from performing the test to designing what should be tested — and from clearing a checklist to catching the test that's technically passing and substantively wrong.

GRC Security + AI

The bigger shift here isn't AI doing security work — it's AI needing security work done to it. SAP landscapes are starting to grant system access to AI agents and automation the same way they grant it to people, which means every SoD ruleset, role design, and access review now has to account for a user that never sleeps and can execute a transaction faster than anyone can review it. Specialists who understand both classical access-risk discipline and how agentic AI actually gets provisioned inside SAP will be the ones deciding what a machine identity is allowed to touch — a genuinely new, durable niche, not a threatened one.

In both cases, the entry-level task shrinks and the judgment task grows — which is exactly why our training teaches judgment first, on real scenarios, not just menu navigation.

OUR DELIVERY METHOD

Watch it done Live. Do it. Prove it.

A live S/4HANA server, a real project scenario, and a training sequence built around one idea: you learn a skill by watching it done correctly once, then doing it yourself until the gap between your work and the model closes.

1

Watch it done, live

An instructor works a real deficiency or access-risk scenario end to end on a live training server — reasoning through what to test and why, before ever opening a transaction.

2

Do it yourself

You repeat the same exercise independently, in your own practice environment, from the same starting scenario — with no script beyond what you already watched.

3

Prove it against a model answer

You write your own conclusion before you ever see ours, then compare the two honestly. The gap between them is exactly what the next lab is for.

Every scenario is built around a realistic company and a specific, plausible failure — a ruleset that stopped watching for a real conflict, a mitigating control nobody ever actually reviewed, a role split that closed one gap and opened another. You're not memorizing SAP menus. You're learning to catch the kind of thing that gets missed in real engagements.
